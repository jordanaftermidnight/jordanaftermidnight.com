BALSAS in one page

BALSAS is open-source firmware that turns an AMYboard v1.5 into an oscillator voice for Eurorack. It replaces the board's stock firmware. It is an unofficial project and is not made or endorsed by the AMYboard makers or Makerfabs.

What it does

  • Eight oscillator shapes (analog, phase modulation, wavefolder, wavetable, phase distortion, resonant, formant, bytebeat), each with two parameters, plus a vocoder and an LFO.
  • Pitch from the knob, from MIDI in (with thru), or from CV in at 1 V per octave. CV out gives the note and a gate.
  • Two modules can be linked for hard sync and FM, over audio, S/PDIF or a link cable.
  • One push encoder and the 128x128 OLED run everything.

Where it stands

  • Version 0.x, a prototype. Two boards have run it on the bench with Eurorack power.
  • The core is portable C++17 with 21 test files that run on a computer. The hardware checklist has results for both boards.
  • Per-board calibration needs a multimeter, and the CV out sweep alone takes about 7 minutes. CV out accuracy on both boards is within about 20 mV from 0 to 5 V.
  • Still open: a longer burn-in, power-glitch tests, an operational guide for building several units, and a printed panel. See docs/roadmap.md.

Who it is for

Eurorack builders who want a digital voice on an inexpensive open board, and people who want a worked example of firmware for the AMYboard hardware that is not the stock AMY synth.

Licences

  • Firmware: GPLv3 or later.
  • Panels: the prototype uses AMYboard's own acrylic front panel. My own panels will be based on its fit, with a separate licence to be set when the files are published.
  • Board: the AMYboard design and its stock software are open source (software MIT). See CREDITS.md.

BALSAS user guide

This guide covers playing the module and setting it up. It is written for version 0.x on the AMYboard v1.5. Calibration has its own document, docs/per-board-calibration.md.

The module is Eurorack format, 10 HP. On the front: the OLED at the top, the push encoder on the left, and ten jacks in two columns on the right. The jacks are labelled on the official panel file: five rows, each with an IN jack on the left and an OUT jack on the right.

The panel

Row IN (left) OUT (right)
digital S/PDIF in S/PDIF out
analog audio in (line in) audio out
MIDI MIDI in (TRS) MIDI out and thru (TRS)
CV1 CV in 1: pitch CV out 1: the note
CV2 CV in 2: gate or timbre CV out 2: gate

The layout and legends follow the AMYboard 10 HP front panel. The encoder sits to the left of the analog row.

What you need

  • An AMYboard v1.5 with the BALSAS firmware flashed (see the README).
  • Eurorack power. USB alone runs the audio, but the output stages have no negative rail without the rack supply, so CV out is wrong on USB power.
  • The four DIP switches on the board set the audio levels. All four toward the opto-isolator is modular level, and the firmware is tuned for that. All four off is line level.

First sound

  1. Patch the audio out to your mixer or amp.
  2. Power the rack. A splash shows the name and version, then the scope appears.
  3. Turn the knob. The note changes and the scope shows the wave.
  4. Hold the knob down and turn to step through the shapes.

Everything you change is saved after about two seconds and comes back at the next power on.

The screen

The scope in the middle is drawn from the oscillator's own samples. The top band shows the shape on the left and the note on the right. The two bars at the bottom are the timbre and color, labelled for the current shape. Small tags appear when something is active:

Tag Where Meaning
M (in a box) top left, under the band MIDI is driving the pitch
CV top left, under the band CV in 1 is driving the pitch
DIG under the CV tag the input is the S/PDIF receiver
VOC under the band, left of centre the vocoder mix is above 0
GATE centre, under the band a gate is present on CV in 2 and the key is down
LFO under the band, between GATE and GRIT the LFO depth is above 0
GRIT top right, under the band the lo-fi effect is on
LINK / FREE centre of the top band a linked module is talking: coupled (LINK) or decoupled (FREE)

The knob

Gesture What it does
Turn Tune. A slow turn moves a semitone per click and a fast turn accelerates
Press and turn Pick the shape
One push Edit timbre. Push again to go back to tune
Two pushes Edit color
Three pushes Grit on or off
Push twice and hold the second push Scale menu. Turn picks the scale, press and turn picks the root, a click or the same gesture leaves, 5 seconds idle leaves
Four pushes LFO screen (rate and depth)
Five pushes Vocoder screen
Long press, about 0.8 seconds Couple or decouple from a linked module. Keep holding to 2 seconds and it is undone and the note returns to C2, so release at once to decouple
Hold 2 seconds Return the note to C2
Push twice and turn while holding the second push Release every held MIDI key, centre the bend, send all-notes-off. Use it when a note sticks after a cable was pulled

Six or more quick pushes count as the vocoder gesture followed by a normal push.

Editing timbre and color

One push highlights the timbre bar and a turn then edits it. Two pushes do the same for the color bar. The highlight clears after about two seconds without a turn.

Every shape has a timbre and a color control. They mean something different in each shape and the screen shows the current names.

The pictures are the firmware's own frames, rendered on a computer with make shots at the default settings (note C3, timbre and color at the middle). The hidden shape appears after you turn forward all the way round the shape list twice.

Scales

The scale menu quantizes the note to a scale and root. The scales are chromatic, major, minor, dorian, pentatonic major and minor, blues, whole tone, harmonic minor, phrygian, lydian and mixolydian. Chromatic means no quantizing. The quantizer applies to the knob and to CV in 1.

Patching

MIDI keyboard to audio. The simplest setup.

Sequencer or keyboard CV and gate. Calibrate the CV input first (see the calibration document).

BALSAS as a controller for other modules. CV out 1 carries the note and CV out 2 the gate. Turn it on with cvout on once the module is calibrated.

Two modules. Module A feeds module B for sync and FM through the analog jacks or S/PDIF (use one), MIDI passes along the thru, and the link cable carries the pitch.

Pitch sources

The module takes pitch from the knob, MIDI or CV in 1.

  • MIDI. Plug a MIDI source into the TRS in jack. The module listens on every channel and follows the keys you play, with pitch bend and the mod wheel. The in jack is passed to the out jack unchanged (thru). If your cable is wired Type B, send midi type b over USB serial once. A held key has no timeout and ends with its note off. A pad that sends aftertouch and then holds still is fine.
  • CV in 1. One volt per octave. 0 V is C0 by default. The knob transposes the CV pitch, with the centre position meaning no change. Calibrate the input first (see below), otherwise the pitch is only close.
  • CV in 2. Three modes: gate, timbre input, or auto-detect (the default). With nothing patched in auto mode the tone plays continuously. Patch a keyboard gate and the first press arms it, after which the tone follows the gate with a short fade in and out. cv2 timbre makes the input move the timbre instead. Do not leave cv2 timbre set on a module with a gate cable in it. cv2 auto returns to detection.

CV out

  • CV out 1 carries the played note, 0 to 5 V, one volt per octave, with 0 V as C0 and 5 V as C5.
  • CV out 2 is a gate, about 5 V while a note is held.

CV out is off by default. Turn it on with cvout on over serial after calibration. The tables are per board, because the DAC differs a little from board to board. The calibration is saved with a checksum. If the saved record is damaged the module refuses to use it, says "calibration REFUSED" in cvout, and needs cvoutcal run again.

Two modules

Two modules can work together in three ways.

  • Audio. Patch module A's audio out into module B's audio in. B can then hard sync to A (sync on) or use A as an FM source (fm on).
  • S/PDIF. Patch A's S/PDIF out into B's S/PDIF in for the same thing in the digital domain. The DIG tag shows when it is present. If the level seems off, dtrim 1.0 sets the digital input drive.
  • Link cable. A cable between the two rear ports carries the pitch. One module becomes master and the other follows, and the long press couples or decouples them. Connect SDA, SCL and ground only. Never connect the 3V3 pin.

The vocoder

Five pushes open the vocoder screen. The line input is the modulator and the oscillator is the carrier. The screen has a shift control for the band spacing. The vocoder is a separate mode from the shapes.

The LFO

Four pushes open the LFO screen. It has a rate and a depth. Depth off means the LFO does nothing. The LFO moves the timbre.

Serial commands

Connect over USB at the usual serial rate and type help for the full list. The ones you use most:

Command What it does

`cvout on off`

`enc invert on off`

The calibration commands (cvtrim, cvmpc, cvoutcal, cvout fix, cvzero, cvidle) are explained in the calibration document.

Flashing and updating

tools/flash.sh <port> flashes without any button presses. Run it twice, because the first run reboots the board into the bootloader. If a board stays silent after failed flashes, unplug and replug USB (hold BOOT and tap RST if it still does not come back). Flashing does not touch saved settings or calibration.

Troubleshooting

What you see What to try
CV out sits at half scale or jumps between two levels Some AMYboard v1.5 DAC batches power up in a half-scale range. This firmware sets the range register at every start, so reflash a board running older firmware, and check it is on Eurorack power
cvout says calibration REFUSED The stored record failed its checksum. Run cvoutcal again
A MIDI note sticks Push twice and turn, or send midi panic
Hum on the MIDI cable The MIDI in sleeve is grounded on this board, which can cause a ground loop (reported upstream as AMYboard issue 1198). Try a different cable or power arrangement
The pitch from CV is a few cents off Redo cvtrim and cvmpc against a meter
No sound on USB power only Use Eurorack power. The outputs need the negative rail
Opening the serial port reset the board It can happen on some hosts. Wait about 5 seconds before sending commands

Limits

  • The CV inputs are read about 170 times a second and move in steps of about 10 cents, so they are not for audio-rate modulation. Use the audio input for audio-rate sync and FM.
  • The vocoder CPU cost is estimated, not measured on every board. cpu shows it with the vocoder on.
  • This is a prototype. See docs/roadmap.md for what is still open.

Build guide

How to build the firmware from source, flash a board, calibrate it and check it. Tested on macOS with two AMYboard v1.5 boards. tools/flash.sh currently looks for the Arduino tools in ~/Library/Arduino15, so on Linux or Windows set that path first or flash with esptool by hand using the addresses in the script.

Tools

  • clang++ and make, for the host tests
  • arduino-cli with the ESP32 core esp32:esp32 version 3.3.12 (arduino-cli core install esp32:esp32@3.3.12)
  • a USB cable, a multimeter and a Eurorack supply for calibration
  • optional: Python 3 and pyserial for tools/e2e_loopback.py and tools/serial_log.py

Get the code

git clone <repository url>
cd voice-firmware

Test on the computer

make test              # every test file
make test T=test_osc   # one file

The core in core/src/vf/ has no chip dependencies, so all of it is tested on the computer. A passing run ends each file with a line like 5 tests, 0 failures, and make exits 0.

Build the firmware

make fw                # development build, with the raw hardware bench commands
make fw RELEASE=1      # the build to hand to other people, without the raw bench commands

The development build is in build/fw, the release build in build/fw-release. The release build leaves out pcmw, test, fault, dacsweep, cvsweep, echotest, pintest, pullcheck and oled. The calibration commands stay in both.

Flash

Find the board's serial port (on macOS something like /dev/cu.usbmodem101), then:

make flash PORT=/dev/cu.usbmodem101
make flash RELEASE=1 PORT=/dev/cu.usbmodem101

make flash builds first and calls tools/flash.sh. No buttons are needed. The first run kicks the running firmware into the bootloader and may report an error, so run it twice. If a board stays silent after failed flashes, unplug and replug USB. If it still does not come back, hold BOOT, tap RST and flash again.

Flashing does not touch the saved settings or the calibration (the NVS partition). To start a unit from scratch, erase that partition on purpose (offset 0x9000, size 0x5000).

tools/backup_flash.sh <port> makes a chunked backup of a board's whole flash. Take one before you replace the stock firmware on a board you want to keep as it came.

Regenerate the screenshots, diagrams and audio

make shots     # OLED screens, docs/img/*.png
make samples   # audio examples, docs/audio/*.mp3 (needs lame)
make guide     # the guides as one HTML file and one PDF in guide/ (needs pandoc and Google Chrome)
python3 tools/guide.py --web <dir>   # a copy for a website: index.html, the PDF and the audio as separate files
python3 tools/diagrams.py docs/img   # panel, patching and block diagrams, docs/img/*.svg

The screens and audio come from the real firmware code run on the computer, so they follow the code. make shots needs Python 3 with Pillow. Run them again after any change to the screens or the voice.

Calibrate

Follow docs/per-board-calibration.md. In short, with Eurorack power and the loopback patch (out 1 to in 1, out 2 to in 2):

  1. cvtrim both CV inputs against the meter.
  2. Optional: cvmpc for a six-point correction of each input.
  3. cvoutcal (about 7 minutes), then cv1 off and cvout on.
  4. Check out 1 with the meter at notes 12, 36 and 72: it should read 0, 2 and 5 V within about 20 mV.

Check

  • boot prints the version and the start-up report.
  • python3 tools/e2e_loopback.py --loopback runs the audio loopback checks with a cable from line out to line in.
  • docs/e2e-checklist.md is the full hand checklist, with the results from the two prototypes.

Sharing units

These boards flash over USB with no signing keys, so anyone can put their own build on a unit with tools/flash.sh. Keep it that way: no secure boot and no locked flash.

Per-board calibration

Every module runs the same firmware image. What differs between two boards is a small set of values stored in flash (NVS), measured on that board. A new build or a second module needs these done once, in this order, with Eurorack power connected (USB alone gives the output stages no negative rail) and a multimeter.

Step What it sets NVS How
1. DIP switches line level (all OFF) or modular level (all ON) none (hardware) all four toward the opto-isolator = modular. The levels in config.h were tuned with all ON
2. CV in trim each CV input's zero and gain (the ADC divider is only about 1% accurate) avcv: z0, z1, m00..m11 patch out 1 to in 1, out 2 to in 2, cvtrim reset, cvout off, dacraw 0 <code for 0 V> and cvtrim 1 0, then a code near 5 V, read the jack with the meter, cvtrim 1 <meter volts>. Same for input 2 with dacraw 1
2b. CV in correction (optional) the ADC's remaining bend after the two-point trim, from a meter avcv: mp0, mp1 cvmpc 1 clear, then for each of about 5 voltages across 0 to 5 V: read what the input says (cvmpc 1 prints it as raw now) and what the meter says on the source jack, and enter cvmpc 1 <read V> <meter V>. Up to 16 points, interpolated linearly. The meter's own resolution (0.01 V is about 12 cents) is the floor. Redo it after any new cvtrim
3. CV out table the DAC code for each volt on out 1, and the gate high/low codes on out 2 avout2: t0, t1, gate codes cv1 on, cv2 timbre, cvoutcal (about 7 minutes; it measures with the step 2b correction applied, so do 2b first; any key typed during the run aborts it and parks both outputs near 0 V), then cv1 off, cvout on
4. Zero note the note a CV of 0 V means (C0 = 12, the same as CV out) avcv: zero cvzero 12
5. Open-jack level what an unplugged CV1 reads avcv: idle cvidle reset (the default is right for these boards)
  1. CV out meter correction (optional): with the loopbacks out, read out 1 at a few notes with the meter and enter what you asked for and what it read: cvout fix 0 0.00, cvout fix 2 1.97, cvout fix 5 4.99. The firmware then asks for the difference (saved in avout2: fix; cvout fix lists the points, cvout fix clear forgets them, and a new cvoutcal clears them). Each point may differ from what was asked by at most 0.1 V, and the output may reach 0.1 V past 0 V and 5 V so a point at the ends takes effect. Without it, board 2 was 30 mV low at note 36 even though its own input said it was right.

Check afterwards with the meter on the unpatched jacks: out 1 at notes 12, 36 and 72 should read 0, 2 and 5 V within about 20 mV, and out 2 about 5 V with the gate high.

Settings that should match on every board: cv1 off, cv2 auto, cvout on, dtrim 1.0, dacgain 1.0, cvzero 12. cv2 timbre pins the timbre at full if a gate cable is patched into CV 2, so do not leave it set on a patched module.

Measured on the two development boards (2026-11-10): the 0 V and 5 V DAC codes were 15911 and 23909 on board 1, 15999 and 24058 on board 2, so the tables really do differ by a few tens of codes.

Why the numbers differ

The two boards need different DAC codes for the same voltage. The 0 V codes are 88 apart (15911 and 15999) and the 5 V codes are 149 apart (23909 and 24058). That is about 55 mV at 0 V and 93 mV at 5 V, so board 1's table on board 2 would be a semitone or more out at the top (a semitone is 83 mV). The span between 0 V and 5 V differs by 61 codes (7998 and 8059), under 1%, so most of the difference is offset. Each board needs its own table. The causes are on the board and cannot be changed in firmware: the DAC's gain and offset, the roughly 1% resistor dividers in front of the ADC, and the output stage.

The amount of hand work is smaller than it looks. cvoutcal measures the output through the module's own input, so only the input trim (step 2) and the optional fix points (step 6) need a meter. Keep each unit's numbers on its calibration sheet.

What the firmware protects

  • The CV out record is saved with a CRC-32. If it fails at boot (a power cut half way through a save, flash damage) the module refuses it, cvout says "calibration REFUSED", and you run cvoutcal again. Older records without a checksum load as they are and get one at their next save.
  • The tables must be monotone and inside the codes cvoutcal sweeps (14000 to 26000). A failed or aborted cvoutcal parks each output at its own 0 V code.
  • Values read back from flash for the CV inputs and the zero note are checked, and a damaged record falls back to the defaults.

Acceptance test

A unit passes when all of these hold after calibration, with Eurorack power and DIPs ON:

Check Limit
Out 1 at notes 12, 36, 60, 72 (0, 2, 4, 5 V) within 20 mV
Out 2 gate high and low about 5 V and about 0 V
CV in 1 and 2, cross-patched from the other module's out pitch within about 25 mV (the meter's floor)
cvout after a power cycle calibration valid, not REFUSED
boot version matches the flashed build, no I2C errors
Audio clean at level 0.5, no clipping on the loopback script

Results from the two prototypes are in docs/e2e-checklist.md. docs/calibration-sheet.md is the sheet to fill in for each unit.
